01

MBCS

Multi-Bay Car Service Centre · My Best Work
View full case study ↗
Role
UI/UX Designer — sole designer
Duration
4-week sprint
Platform
Enterprise SaaS · Desktop
Industry
Automotive After-Sales
Team
Solo — with stakeholders, workshop managers & AI-assisted prototyping
Goal
Reduce workshop delays by improving real-time operational visibility
Tools
ChatGPT (Strategist), AI Studio (Mechanic), Claude (Engineer), GitHub (Garage). Four AI tools, one job each. I made every decision.
Status
Concept case study

Problem

The service centre lacks a unified operational view that enables managers to monitor workflow, identify bottlenecks, and prioritize work effectively. This lack of visibility results in blocked bays, delayed decisions, missed pickup commitments, and reduced workshop throughput.

What I did

Business understanding → Research → Journey mapping → Decision mapping → IA → Wireframes → Workflow design → Interaction design → Design system → High fidelity prototype

Key decision

  1. How I reframed the product when every system said "track jobs"Why it matteredI spent the first week designing the wrong product.The decisionThat's when I changed my approach. I stopped designing around the job and started designing around the flow of work across the floor. A job marked "in progress" tells you nothing. What matters is whether work is moving, where it's stuck, and what that's about to cost. Every later decision comes back to this one.ResultFrom that point on, it wasn't "another tracking screen." It was a tool for controlling flow. The biggest decision of the project happened before I'd designed a single final screen — deciding what the product was actually for.

Design targets

Before the board vs the targets it was built to hit.

Blocked-bay time42 min → 12 min
On-time pickup74% → 95%
Spot a bottleneck15 min → <1 min
Allocation3 min → 30 sec

Outcome & learning

I started by designing pages. I ended up designing decisions. That shift changed how I approach product design. I design around operational problems first, then the flow, then the screens.

02

Goibibo

Flight Booking · A Difficult Problem
View full case study ↗
Role
Senior UX Designer
Timeline
8 Weeks
Platform
Goibibo Mobile App
Industry
Travel & Tourism
Research
3 users, 2 scenarios each
Status

Problem

Users couldn't compare without losing context. Users scrolled 4-6 times to remember fare details. 87 seconds spent comparing before decision. The problem wasn't lack of information — it was information architecture.

What I did

If we allow users to compare fares side-by-side instead of scrolling vertically, they will make faster, more confident decisions. Built on 3 core principles: Context, Comparison, Confidence.

Key decision

  1. Fare Comparison StructureContextThe problem wasn't the content. It was the mental load. Users had to remember details from one fare while reading another, turning comparison into a memory test.The solutionRedesigned into a horizontal comparison structure where users could see all fares side-by-side with key attributes clearly highlighted.

Usability test results

Test with 3 users, 2 scenarios each.

Result0% confusion
Result42% faster decisions
Result34% more confident

Outcome & learning

This project taught me that great UX isn't about adding features — it's about removing friction. Every scroll avoided, every second saved, every confident decision — that's what good design looks like.
